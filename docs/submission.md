# Submission drafts (web form, due 3:30 PM 2026-09-26)

Character limits are from the participant guide. Problem areas: Water Safety and Water Delivery. Prototype link: https://hack4humanity2026.github.io/remotewater/

## Brief description (max 250)

June 2026: E. coli at Inukjuak's water plant. 1,800 people told to boil, no end date, because nobody knew which truckload was in which tank. RemoteWater logs every load and delivery, so a failed test tells each home in minutes, offline, in Inuktitut.

## How it addresses the problem (max 700)

Inukjuak has no pipes. Water is tested at the plant, then trucked to a tank in each home with no record of which water went where. RemoteWater turns existing steps into a chain of custody: the operator's daily chlorine reading becomes the batch, the driver taps Delivered at each house (offline, syncing at each refill), and the lab records pass or fail against the batch. The minute a result fails, every load since the last clean sample is traced to the homes it reached, school and clinic first. One approved notice goes out at once: the home status card, the village Facebook group, the radio script, and a driver visit list for homes with no phone. Each home clears when clean water arrives.

## Social impact (max 700)

1,821 people in Inukjuak, and every Nunavik village, drink trucked water. Today a bad result means everyone boils for an unknown time, and the news travels by radio, the Facebook group and word of mouth, with no list of who has heard. RemoteWater tells each household the status of its own tank within minutes of the lab call, in the language 96% of the village speaks at home, posts the same notice to the Facebook group and radio, and reaches homes without a phone through the driver who visits every day or two. Advisories end house by house as clean water arrives, not village-wide weeks later. The health centre and school are told first. Operators get a record for the investigation.

## Unique value (max 700)

Most water-safety ideas put a sensor in every tank. Sensors need calibration and a technician, and Inukjuak has neither. RemoteWater applies food safety's batch-and-lot model to a water truck. The truck is the network, syncing at each refill, so latency is minutes with no cell signal, on home WiFi alone. Radio, the Facebook group and a knock on the door are first-class channels beside the app, so an outage never silences a notice. Nothing is installed at any house. It scales by file, not hardware: one static site, one buildings file per village, run regionally by the office that already compiles every village's weekly tests. Every number is labelled sourced, assumed or hypothetical.
