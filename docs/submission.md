# Submission drafts (web form, due 3:30 PM 2026-09-26)

Character limits are from the participant guide. Problem areas: Water Safety and Water Delivery. Prototype link: https://hack4humanity2026.github.io/remotewater/

## Brief description (max 250)

June 2026: E. coli at Inukjuak's water plant. 1,800 people told to boil, no end date, because nobody knew which truckload was in which tank. RemoteWater logs every load and delivery, so a failed test tells each home in minutes, offline, in Inuktitut.

## How it addresses the problem (max 700)

Inukjuak has no pipes. Water is tested at the plant, then trucked to a tank in each home with no record of which water went where. RemoteWater closes that gap with what already exists: the operator's daily chlorine reading becomes the batch, the driver taps Delivered at each house (offline, syncing at the plant), and the lab records pass or fail against the batch. A failed result traces every load since the last clean sample to the homes it reached, priority buildings first. One approved notice then goes out on every channel the village uses: the home status card, Facebook, FM radio, and the driver's next visit for homes without a phone. Each home clears itself when post-clean water arrives.

## Social impact (max 700)

1,821 people in Inukjuak, and every Nunavik village, drink trucked water. When something goes wrong today, everyone boils, nobody knows for how long, and the news travels by radio and word of mouth. RemoteWater tells each household the status of its own tank the minute a result is known, in the language 96% of the village speaks at home, and reaches homes with no phone through the driver who already visits every day or two. Advisories end house by house instead of village-wide weeks later. The school and the health centre, which close when water service stops, are notified first. Operators get a searchable delivery record for the investigation. Nothing is installed at any house.

## Unique value (max 700)

Most water-safety ideas put a sensor in every tank. Sensors need calibration and a technician, and Inukjuak has neither. RemoteWater borrows the batch-and-lot model food safety has used for decades and applies it to a water truck: the truck is the network, syncing at the plant several times a day, so it works with home WiFi only and no signal on the road. It treats FM radio and a knock on the door as first-class channels beside the app, so an internet outage never silences a notice. It is built on real data: OpenStreetMap buildings, the 2021 census, Quebec's sampling rules and the June 2026 reports, with every number labelled sourced, assumed or hypothetical so the community can correct it.
