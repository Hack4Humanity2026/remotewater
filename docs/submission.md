Character limits are from the participant guide. Counts are shown after each block; edit freely.

## Brief description (max 250)

RemoteWater treats trucked water like food: every truck fill is a batch, every delivery is logged offline, and when a lab sample fails each home is told by SMS, in its own language, whether the water in its tank is affected and when it is clear.

## How it addresses the problem (max 700)

In Inukjuak water is tested at the plant, then trucked to a tank in each home with no record of which water went where. When E. coli was found at the loading arm in June 2026, the whole village was told to boil with no end date. RemoteWater gives the driver a two-tap app that works with no signal: "filled at plant" (with a chlorine reading that blocks low loads) and "delivered". The plant computer syncs when the truck returns. A failed result traces every load since the last clean sample to the homes it reached, sends a boil text to each in Inuktitut, English or French, and clears each home the moment post-clean water reaches its tank. Nothing to install at the house.

## Social impact (max 700)

Nunavik's 14 villages all rely on trucked water, and Inukjuak alone has had shortages or advisories in 2022, 2024 and 2026. Today an advisory covers the whole village and has no end date for anyone. RemoteWater tells every household within minutes, in the language 96% of Inukjuak speaks at home, names the delivery in their own tank, and ends the advisory house by house instead of village-wide. It also gives the operator and the health board a delivery record that makes the next investigation faster. It runs on phones people already have, sends SMS that need no data plan, and the whole app is a few hundred kilobytes that works offline.

## Unique value (max 700)

Most water-safety ideas put a sensor in the tank. Sensors need calibration and a technician, and Inukjuak has neither. RemoteWater instead borrows the batch-and-lot model food safety has used for decades and applies it to a water truck. The truck itself is the network, because it returns to the plant many times a day. The only instrument is a chlorine strip at the loading arm, which also gives an early warning days before the Montreal lab. It is built on real data: OpenStreetMap buildings, the 2021 census, Quebec's RQEP rules and the June 2026 Nunatsiaq report. Every number on screen is labelled sourced, assumed or hypothetical, so the community can correct it.
